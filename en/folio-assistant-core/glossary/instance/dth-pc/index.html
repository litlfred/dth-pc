Country Trade Item Identifier candidate

Candidate definitions (2)
  1. Trade Item (Glossary of the Digital Transformation Handbook for Health Product Catalogue)

    Trade items are manufacturer-specific branded health products that are identifiable and unique to a particular brand or manufacturer.

    Handbook wording, quoted (Ch 2, page 28). A candidate: not adopted. Owner ruling 2026-10-31: Trade Item is the WHO concept, with this narrow sense; the GS1 trade item is a separate external concept and the WHO term's broadMatch (every WHO Trade Item is a GS1 trade item, not the reverse: GS1's also covers services). The handbook's glossary (page 9) prints the GS1 wording, 'Products or services that are priced, ordered or invoiced at any point in the supply chain', citing GS1 (its reference 13); that sentence is GS1's definition, recorded here as a note, not as this concept's. litlfred/smart-ra#142 item 1.

  2. National Product Catalogue (Glossary of the Digital Transformation Handbook for Health Product Catalogue)

    Adopting a suitable technology tool supports the ongoing management of product master data while maintaining consistent quality and compliance with defined standards. A widely used term for such a tool is the NPC, which many countries implement to oversee product master data.

    Handbook wording, quoted. A candidate: not adopted, nothing chosen between alternatives. The handbook names the TOOL the NPC; it realises the Product Registry foundational component of the dth-sc DHSC architecture (page 16). Link target in smart-ra: Product Catalog Management System / Product Registry.

Generic Product candidate

In this handbook, the terms ‘generic product,’ ‘product,’ or ‘product information’ pertain to the general identifying details of a health product, excluding any manufacturer-specific information.

Handbook wording, quoted. A candidate: not adopted, nothing chosen between alternatives. Alternative wording in Ch 2 (page 28): 'Generic products can be described as generic items that conceal the brand or manufacturer information.' Countries identify them with generic national product codes.
